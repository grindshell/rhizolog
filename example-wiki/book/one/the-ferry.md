# The Ferry

There is a kiosk on the pier with a hand-written card taped inside the glass,
and the card has been right about the weather more often than the forecast has.
The ferry was late, and being late was the only thing it had ever been. The
woman behind the glass said so without looking up, in the tone of somebody who
has given one answer for thirty years and no longer expects it to help.

Marren ran the crossing, and had run it since his father stopped. He took the
fares in a tin, counted them twice, and handed back coins he had plainly been
carrying since the previous week. I asked how long we might be. He considered
the water for a while, which I understood eventually to be a reply rather than
a rudeness. It was Marren who said, in the end, that we would go on the tide,
and that was the closest anybody came to naming a time.

Marren was not being difficult. The sea was simply not ready yet, and no card
taped inside any glass was going to hurry it.
